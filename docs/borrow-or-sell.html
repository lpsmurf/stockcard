Borrow or sell?

Borrowing against what you own keeps it invested and skips a taxable sale. But the tax only moves to later and the loan charges interest. This page shows when borrowing really wins, with the numbers.

Stocks, 1–3 yearsSelling is usually cheaper

At 9.9–14.9% APR. Borrowing wins below a ~5–7% rate, or for short loans repaid from income.

Watches, cards, artAbout even, and you keep it

Can't sell 10% of a watch; selling costs 10–25%. Banks don't lend against them.

Our pitchCash in minutes, without selling

Not "cheaper than tax". Strongest for collectibles, on-chain stocks and short needs.

The real advantages

Why people borrow instead of selling

Private banks have offered this for decades as Lombard loans. The benefits are real, but they're not equally strong.

You keep the thing itself Very strong · collectibles

You can't sell 10% of a Daytona or a PSA 10 card. A €15k loan keeps the €60k watch yours, safely in the vault.

No selling costs Strong · collectibles, art

Stocks cost ~0.1–0.5% to sell. Watch dealers buy well below retail, card marketplaces take fees, and art auctions often cost 20%+ in commissions and premiums.

No taxable sale now Depends on country

A loan isn't income or a sale, so capital gains tax isn't triggered. It's postponed until you sell, not cancelled. In the Netherlands there's no tax on the sale at all.

No forced timing Strong

Need money in a dip? Selling locks in the loss. Borrowing lets you wait, as long as the fall doesn't reach liquidation.

Speed Strong

One signature to the card, SEPA Instant to your bank in seconds. Selling stocks settles T+1, then a transfer; selling art takes weeks or months.

Keep the upside and income Depends on APR

Everything stays invested and dividends keep accruing. It only pays off if the assets grow faster than the loan costs.

Flexible repayment Moderate

No schedule, no early-repayment fee, interest only on what you've drawn.

No credit check (for now) Temporary

The collateral secures the loan. The EU Consumer Credit Directive 2023/2225 applies from 20 Nov 2026 and brings creditworthiness rules. Don't lead with this.

What the model shows

The catch: interest, liquidation, and tax that only moves

If you'll sell eventually anyway, the loan has to cost less than your assets earn.

For a stock portfolio expected to return 7% a year, that break-even APR is about 5–7%. If prices fall, having sold earlier always wins. A liquidation also realises the gain, so the tax arrives anyway.

Where borrowing clearly wins

  • Short bridges repaid from income. 6 months on €40k at 12.9% is ~€2,590 of interest, against €7,229 of tax and trading costs today, with 27% of the portfolio pulled out of the market.
  • Things you can't split or that cost a lot to sell: watches, graded cards, art.
  • Low APRs around 3.7–6%.
  • Decades-long holders, where deferral compounds.

Where selling wins

  • Loans that run for years at 10%+ on stocks.
  • Falling markets: the sold part didn't fall.
  • No tax on sale (Netherlands box 3), unless it's a collectible or speed matters.
  • Belgium's annual exemption: spreading small sales across years can beat a loan.

Two traps we must clear before launch

1. In some countries, moving tokens into a smart contract can itself count as a sale (UK guidance on DeFi lending looks at beneficial ownership). We need a written tax opinion per country. 2. Loan interest for a car or a house deposit is usually not tax-deductible for individuals. Never imply it is. Tokenized stocks can also be taxed differently from shares.

The new Send-to-bank feature

Car and house: borrow straight to your IBAN

One signature borrows USDC and sends it to a payout address; the provider converts to EUR and pays your bank by SEPA Instant. In the demo the SEPA leg is simulated; in production it's a Bridge liquidation address routed to SEPA.

Car

  • Good fit: €15–60k, often repaid from salary within 1–2 years.
  • Compare: Dutch banks advertise car loans from 7.5% (ABN AMRO) and 9.9–11.9% (ING). Our 9.9–12.9% is in range, with no schedule and no sale.
  • Cheaper elsewhere: broker securities loans (IBKR EUR ~3.7%, German online brokers ~4.9–7%).

House deposit

  • Works for short bridges, e.g. until an old home is sold.
  • Mortgage lenders ask where the deposit comes from and count other debts; a loan-funded deposit can cut what they lend. Check with an adviser first (in NL, also BKR).
  • Large payouts get AML checks; the app asks for a purpose above €10,000.
  • Big amounts, big stakes: keep the suggested max 35% LTV in view.

What this means for StockCard

Where we're clearly valuable

  • Collectibles, watches, art. No bank Lombard for normal customers; the alternatives are selling or pawn-style loans that typically cost far more than 15.9% a year.
  • On-chain stock holders. xStocks and SPCX holders have no broker to borrow from.
  • Speed and spend. Card or IBAN in minutes, 24/7.

Where we're weak

  • Stock holders with a broker can borrow at ~3.7–7%. At 9.9–14.9% we can't claim to beat the tax for them.
  1. Change the pitch line

    "Get cash from what you own in minutes, without selling it." Use tax only with the caveat "Selling can trigger tax. Borrowing postpones it but costs interest." Never "tax-free" or "cheaper than tax".

  2. Put this calculator in the app

    On the Send-to-bank sheet above €10,000, with the member's country. It builds trust and helps with consumer-credit duties.

  3. Consider a "Prime" stock band

    About 6.9–7.9% at ≤ 20% LTV would make the tax story true for many cases. Savers get ~6%, so it needs cheaper liquidity, a lower-APY tranche or subscription revenue; rerun the unit economics first.

  4. Keep the margin in collectibles and art

    11.9–15.9% is competitive there and the member benefit is largest.

  5. Get two opinions before launch

    Tax (deposit into the vault as a disposal; tokenized-stock treatment) and consumer credit (CCD2 from 20 Nov 2026).
