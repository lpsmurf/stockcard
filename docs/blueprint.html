Engineering blueprint · Stocklana hackathon

Spend what you own. Never sell it.

StockCard lets someone who owns tokenized stocks, art notes or graded collectibles lock them up, get a dollar credit line against them, and pay with a virtual card. They repay whenever they like and take their assets back. This page explains how the MVP will work, in plain words, before any of it gets built.

CHAPTER 01

The idea in one picture

It works like a pawn shop with a debit card, except the "shop" is a public program on Solana that nobody can quietly change, and the owner can always see exactly where their assets are.

Why people would use it

Selling stock to pay for something triggers taxes and gives up future gains. Borrowing against it keeps the position. Nexo does this for crypto; nobody does it for stocks with a card on Solana.

Who already does part of it

Kamino and Jupiter Lend accept xStocks, but as trading dashboards with no card. Backpack lends against 2 stocks inside its exchange. ether.fi Cash has a borrow card, but only for crypto collateral.

What judges ask

"Could this be a real app people actually use?" So the demo must run end to end on devnet: deposit, borrow, a card purchase that really moves USDC, repay.

CHAPTER 02

Plain-word glossary

The rest of this page uses these words. Each one gets a one-line everyday meaning.

Wallet Phantom, Solflare
The user's app that holds their tokens and approves ("signs") every action. StockCard never holds the user's keys.
Tokenized stock xStocks, SPCX
A token on Solana backed 1:1 by a real share held by the issuer. NVDAx follows NVIDIA's share price.
Program smart contract
The rulebook that runs on Solana. It holds the vaults and does all the money math. The app can't override it.
Vault PDA account
A locked box owned by the program. Collateral goes in one vault per asset; lendable USDC sits in the pool vault.
USDC stablecoin
A digital dollar. It's what users borrow and what the card spends.
Oracle price signer, Switchboard
Whoever puts prices on-chain. Ours posts public stock prices every minute; the program refuses to lend if a price is too old.
LTV loan-to-value
Debt divided by collateral value. $500 borrowed against $2,000 of stock is 25% LTV.
Haircut
A safety discount on an asset's price. A 20% haircut counts a $10 art note as $8.
Liquidation
If the price falls too far, anyone may repay part of the debt and receive some collateral at a 5% discount. That keeps the pool whole.
Delegate card limit
A permission the user signs: "the card may spend up to $500 of my USDC". The card can't take more.
Devnet
Solana's free practice network. Tokens have no real value, so the demo is safe to break.
Mock MockBadge
A stand-in for the real thing (test mints, sandbox card, demo prices). Every mock is labeled in the app.

Decided: protect first, liquidate last

1 · Buffer when borrowing

The Borrow sheet suggests staying at or below 35% and shows the liquidation price: "Liquidation if NVDA falls below $153.85 (−27.4%)" for a $1,000 loan. At $750 the stock could fall 45.6%.

2 · Alerts and web push

From 55% LTV the app warns in-app and pushes to the phone, with exact one-tap fixes: add 3.48 NVDAx or repay $258.14. Only one push per band, so nobody gets spammed.

3 · Liquidation

If nobody acts, one 50% liquidation after a −30% crash brings LTV from 67.4% to 52.2%. That's out of danger (amber). The spec's test now says "no longer liquidatable".

Stocks · 50% LTV

NVDAx, SPYx, TSLAx, SPCX. Live market prices from xStocks and Jupiter, posted every minute. When markets are closed, older prices get an extra 10% haircut (20% for SPCX).

Art notes · 30% LTV

TIDE, a placeholder Luxembourg art note. Priced by a signed appraisal, 20% haircut, expires after 100 days.

Pokémon cards · 40% LTV

A graded PSA 10 Pokémon card (devnet mock), valued like Collector Crypt's insured values. Signed partner value, 25% haircut, expires after 8 days.

CHAPTER 06

Architecture: three places code runs

The user's device, a small StockCard server, and the Solana program. Money rules live only in the program. The server holds no user collateral; it can only spend what a user explicitly allowed the card to spend.

One codebase, two platforms

The website is installable as an app. For Android it gets wrapped as an APK with solana-mobile webshell, and wallets connect through Mobile Wallet Adapter. Every screen must work at 360 px wide.

Card provider is swappable

The card code talks to one CardProvider interface. The mock provider (default) does a real devnet USDC transfer; the Bridge provider uses Stripe's card sandbox when keys arrive. Rain or Baanx could plug in later.

CHAPTER 09

Safety and honesty

Judges will poke at risk. These are the rules the code enforces and the risks we disclose rather than hide.

Enforced by the program

Fresh price or no loan. Borrow, withdraw and liquidate all check the price's age in the same transaction. If the two price sources disagree by more than 2%, no new price is posted, so borrowing pauses instead of using a bad price.

Interest first. Interest is added before any change, so nobody can dodge it with timing.

Whole-number math. Cents and basis points only, overflow-checked, rounded in the pool's favor.

The app only previews. Even a hacked app can't approve an unsafe loan.

Risks we disclose

Issuers can pause, freeze or claw back. Real xStocks and SPCX tokens give the issuer those powers. The program checks vault balances and pause flags and blocks new borrows if anything's off.

Stocks don't trade 24/7. Outside market hours older prices are accepted, but with an extra haircut.

Stock splits change balances. xStocks use a multiplier on the token; collateral is valued as tokens × multiplier × price.

Everything is a mock on devnet. Test mints, sandbox card, placeholder artworks, demo prices, all labeled. No partnerships are claimed.

CHAPTER 10

Can we also enter "Best Use of Meteora DBC"?

Yes, but only in one specific way. Meteora's Dynamic Bonding Curve is a launch pool: the price climbs along a preset curve as people buy, and once enough USDC is raised the pool "graduates" into a normal Meteora DAMM v2 trading pool.

The constraint that decides it

A DBC pool always creates a new token (the SDK's createPool needs a fresh baseMint keypair). So DBC can't launch or trade existing xStocks or SPCX; Backed and Backpack issue those. StockCard does, however, plan to issue its own asset: the TIDE art note.

Cost

About 1 to 1.5 extra days: a config + pool script on devnet (same program ID as mainnet), a launch screen with curve progress and a buy button, and a watcher that turns on the TIDE market after graduation.

Tension

Meteora says "working code on mainnet beats slides." A mainnet art-note sale with a placeholder artwork breaks our honest-demo rule and is a securities question. A mainnet-safe alternative is a read-only issuer monitor for existing DBC pools.

Suggested placement

A cuttable Phase 7c after US1–US4, taking the slot of art visuals (T041). Confirm first that Stocklana allows one project to enter sponsor tracks too.

CHAPTER 12

Decide before building

Drawing this blueprint surfaced a few conflicts and open choices. Three are settled; the rest are still open. Each one changes code, so it's cheaper to settle now than on Wednesday.

1 · How liquidation risk is handled

Decided Sept 14

Protect first, liquidate last. A 35% suggested borrow limit with the liquidation price shown; alerts from 55% LTV in-app and through web push, with exact add-stock or repay amounts; if nobody acts, a −30% crash plus one 50% liquidation brings 67.4% down to 52.2%. The acceptance test now reads "no longer liquidatable".

Spec updatedspec.md US3 + FR-042–045, parameters.md §4 bands and push copy, wireframes S2/S3, api.md push routes, tasks T056–T061.

2 · Signed prices for the demo

Decided Sept 14

Every demo market uses an admin-set price, so the crash works on all three asset types: NVDAx ("Demo price"), the TIDE art note ("Appraisal") and a graded PSA 10 Pokémon card ("Partner value"). A live stock price shows beside NVDAx once an oracle is available.

Spec updatedparameters.md markets + oracle table, spec.md assumptions, tasks T029.

3 · Live stock prices

Decided Sept 14

Pyth equity access costs about $2,500/month and Chainlink Data Streams starts at $150/month per feed, so the MVP uses a hybrid: a price signer posts free xStocks + Jupiter prices (both agreed within 0.03% on Sept 14), and a 2-hour Switchboard trial on Wednesday may make SPYx and TSLAx oracle-signed. NVDAx, the art note and the Pokémon card stay signed. Chainlink is the stated production path.
