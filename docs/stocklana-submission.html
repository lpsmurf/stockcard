Project name

StockCard

Tagline / one-liner

Spend what you own. Never sell it. A credit card backed by your stocks, on Solana.

Short description

StockCard is a self-custodial credit line for stock holders. Lock tokenized US stocks (xStocks, Backpack's SPCX) in an on-chain vault, borrow USDC up to a conservative LTV at a fixed APR, and spend it with a virtual card. Card cashback automatically buys more of your stock and adds it to your collateral. Repay anytime, withdraw your stock — you never trigger a sale.

Long description

StockCard is a credit card backed by your stocks — Nexo, but for equities, self-custodial, on Solana. THE PROBLEM People who hold stocks face a bad trade-off when they need cash: sell (lose the position, trigger taxes, miss upside) or wait days for a broker margin loan. DeFi lending exists, but it's built for crypto natives, not stock holders. WHAT IT DOES - Lock tokenized stocks in an on-chain vault (Anchor program, devnet) - Borrow USDC instantly up to a max LTV at a fixed APR — credit-line style, borrow/repay freely - Spend through a virtual card (issuer-agnostic CardProvider interface; a real devnet USDC transfer settles each purchase to a merchant address) - Card cashback buys more of your chosen stock and deposits it into your collateral position - Send-to-bank: borrow USDC and route it to a SEPA payout (simulated rail on devnet) - Position protection: warning bands at 55/60/65% LTV, alert banners and web push with exact add-collateral or repay amounts, and a live "crash price" admin demo (−30%) WHY SOLANA The collateral, the loan and the card settlement are all real on-chain transactions on one network. Tokenized equities (xStocks are Token-2022 with Scaled UI Amount) already trade on Solana — StockCard is the consumer credit layer that makes holding them better than a brokerage account. Sub-second LTV enforcement at instruction level is only practical on Solana. HOW IT'S BUILT - Anchor 1.2 program: Config / Market / Position / SignedPrice PDAs. Every borrow, withdraw and liquidate re-checks LTV against a fresh signed price inside the same instruction — collateral safety lives in the program, not the client. All math is checked integer math, u128 intermediates, interest rounded up. - Token-2022 collateral: xStocks-style mints with the Scaled UI Amount multiplier; positions value raw amount × multiplier × price × (1 − haircut), matching how real xStocks behave (issuer controls, permanent delegate, freeze authority). - Price signer: a server-side oracle posts a signed price only when at least two of {issuer API (xStocks/Backpack), Jupiter, Pyth Hermes} agree within 200 bps. A third source lets it drop a single outlier instead of going dark and keeps pricing alive through an issuer API outage. Equity prices deliberately go stale when the market closes. - App: one Next.js 16 PWA is both the website and the Android app (solana-mobile webshell, Mobile Wallet Adapter). Calm consumer-fintech UI, not a DeFi dashboard. - Savers: a USDC savings pool funds the loans (target ~6% APY to savers, spread to the protocol). WHAT'S REAL VS MOCK (honest demo) - Real: all collateral vault, borrow, repay, liquidate, savings and cashback-deposit transactions; the devnet USDC transfers for card settlement and payouts; signed-price oracle with three real data sources. - Mocked and labelled as such in the UI: the card issuer (mock provider — Bridge sandbox integration is built but gated), the SEPA rail (mock payout address), and the devnet stock/USDC mints (real xStocks have no devnet mints). No real KYC. Track: Credit & yield / Consumer. Also submitted for the Pyth bounty — see below.

Pyth bounty paragraph (Best use of Pyth market data)

Pyth is wired into StockCard's collateral price signer as the third independent source (app/src/lib/prices/pyth.ts, Hermes client). For every price post, the signer requires at least two of {issuer API, Jupiter, Pyth} to agree within 200 bps; Pyth breaks ties, lets the signer drop a single outlier instead of going dark, and covers an issuer API outage — so a lending protocol can keep pricing collateral safely when one feed fails. Pyth never prices collateral alone by design. Prize consideration: lending/collateral product where live financial data does real work.
